EXP-005: Babai alone versus Babai plus QAOA
Internal research explanation, not final poster prose.

Purpose and provenance
  This is a post-hoc reanalysis of all 64 EXP-001 toy Module-LWE examples using
  saved EXP-004 angles, pruning masks, and 524,288 measured bitstrings. No
  parameter refitting, new shots, hardware execution, or sample filtering.
  Run: experiments/runs/EXP-005_20261020T041426326139+0530

Returned answer
  Start with LLL-Babai. Among the N sampled candidates accept a new answer only
  for a strictly smaller ORIGINAL cost. On a tie retain the earlier incumbent.
  The final cost is min(Babai cost, sampled costs). The returned distance is
  its square root. Non-worsening follows from retaining Babai, so strict
  improvements must be measured separately.

Objective and relation to Lv et al.
  Our squared distance is ||q*w + A*s - t||^2 + ||s||^2.
  It is the distance in the augmented secret-and-residual lattice, and is not
  simply the LWE residual norm or the exact CBD negative log likelihood.
  Lv et al., section 5.1, compare residual distance before and after QAOA on
  a different LWE formulation: https://doi.org/10.3390/e24101428
  We perform the analogous quality-refinement comparison, not a reproduction
  of their instances, encoding, noise distribution, or QAOA tuning procedure.
  Closer under our objective does not necessarily mean correct secret recovery.

Expectation calculation
  For each instance, group the exact ideal sampling probabilities by original
  cost, capped at Babai's cost. If S(v)=Pr[capped sampled cost >= v], the minimum
  of N independent samples equals v with probability S(v)^N-S(next v)^N.
  Weight these probabilities by sqrt(v) to obtain expected returned distance,
  and by v to obtain expected squared distance. These are different quantities:
  E[sqrt(cost)] must not be replaced with sqrt(E[cost]).
  The result is independently checked using the survival-sum expectation.
  Strict-improvement probability is 1-(1-p_better)^N. Exact-optimum success is
  one when Babai is optimal, otherwise 1-(1-p_optimum)^N.
  Saved measurements are rescored directly and verified against original-basis
  lattice vectors. Their observed outcomes remain separate from expectations.

Main findings at N=1024, all 64 examples
  Method                  Mean expected distance  Expected optima  Observed optima
  Babai alone                   2.769118             53.0000          53
  + full fixed-angle QAOA        2.731435             55.6205          55
  + weak 25% pruning             2.732418             55.5693          56
  + weak 50% pruning             2.727595             55.7463          57
  + classical 1/2-coordinate     2.685261             61.0000          61
  Exact CVP reference            2.643128             64.0000          --
  Expected counts are repeated-batch averages, not fractional observed cases.
  All QAOA rows above reuse the same saved global angles. Full tuned QAOA and
  10% pruning / global-retrained variants are also reported in summary.json.

Where the improvements happen
  Babai already attains an exact optimum in 53/64 cases, leaving only 11 that
  can improve. Full, 25%-pruned, and 50%-pruned QAOA each strictly improved four
  cases in the saved batch, tied Babai on 60, and worsened none. Of those four,
  two, three, and four respectively reached an exact optimum. These are single
  batches, so the apparent ranking is not reliable from counts alone.
  Exact expected strict improvement counts: 3.9778, 3.9025, and 4.2574 of 64.
  Conditional on the 11 improvable cases, the mean expected distance decreases
  5.91%, 5.75%, and 6.51%, respectively. Across ALL 64, reductions are only
  1.36%, 1.33%, and 1.50%. The conditional view never replaces the full cohort.
  Full QAOA closes about 29.91% of Babai's total distance gap to the known
  optima; 25% pruning closes 29.13% and 50% pruning closes 32.96%.
  The classical 1/2-coordinate refinement improves nine cases, repairs eight
  Babai failures, and closes 66.56% of the available distance gap.
  Uniform sampling of the identical binary box gives only 0.3366 expected
  strict improvements and 53.1858 expected optima. Beating this weak control
  does not establish superiority over classical local refinement.

Concrete saved observation
  For MLWE-009, Babai has squared cost 17 (distance 4.1231). The 25%-pruned
  samples include a candidate with cost 7 (distance 2.6458), the exact optimum.
  The full-QAOA batch reaches cost 16 on this case, while the 50%-pruned batch
  does not improve it. This illustrates shot variability, not a selected
  success criterion or a claim that 25% pruning always wins.

Interpretation and limits
  Yes, QAOA refinement improves Babai's quality on these examples, in the
  qualitative sense used by Lv et al. The effect is modest at the current shot
  budget. It is not evidence of practical superiority over classical methods.
  The 25% pruning candidate preserves most of full QAOA's average refinement
  benefit while reducing all-to-all compiled CX count by 25%. Its slightly
  WORSE mean hybrid quality is relative to full QAOA; it is still BETTER than
  Babai alone. This clarifies the reference point in methodology_EXP004.txt.
  Noisy hardware behavior, end-to-end speed, secure parameters, and unseen
  instance generalization remain untested. The 50% result remains secondary.
