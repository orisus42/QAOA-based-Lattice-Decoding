EXP-001 MATHEMATICAL SPECIFICATION — internal research explanation

1. Toy Module-LWE observation

Use polynomials in R_q = Z_q[x]/(x^n + 1), with n=2, q=17, module rank k=2.
A is a k-by-k matrix of polynomials. s and e are vectors of k polynomials.
Generate t = A*s + e mod q. Each coefficient of s and e is sampled independently
as the sum of eta fair bits minus another eta fair bits, here eta=2.

For a two-coefficient polynomial, x^2=-1. Thus multiplication by a0+a1*x is
the integer matrix [[a0,-a1],[a1,a0]]. Replacing every polynomial entry of A
by its corresponding block gives A_expanded, a 4-by-4 integer matrix.

Relation to ML-KEM: this preserves the negacyclic polynomial-module equation
and a centered-binomial secret/error family. Real ML-KEM uses degree 256,
modulus 3329, and specified parameter sets and algorithms. This experiment
does not implement the KEM, NTT representation, encoding, or secure parameters.
Primary reference: NIST FIPS 203, sections 2.3, 3.2, 4.2.2 and 5.1:
https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/FIPS/NIST.FIPS.203.pdf

2. Explicit lattice objective

In expanded coordinates let d=n*k=4. Define the column basis and target

      B = [q I_d   A_expanded]         y = [t]
          [  0         I_d  ]             [0]

For integer coordinates z=[w;s], minimize

  ||Bz-y||^2 = ||q*w + A_expanded*s - t||^2 + ||s||^2.

For the generated secret and error, w=(t-e-A_expanded*s)/q is integral.
The planted lattice point has residual [-e;s] and squared cost ||e||^2+||s||^2.
B is full rank with determinant q^d. The lattice dimension is 2d=8.

Including the secret coordinates matters. With square A invertible mod q,
the lattice generated by [A | qI] alone is Z^d, making the closest vector to
integer t trivial. That formulation loses the short-secret requirement.

The chosen squared norm is a useful explicit optimization target, but is NOT
the exact negative log likelihood of the centered-binomial distribution.
It permits integer secrets outside the sampling support. Consequently, the
generated secret is not guaranteed to be a CVP optimum. This is measured,
not assumed. No minimum-distance computation or BDD certificate is claimed.

3. Classical references and correctness

Compute Babai's nearest-plane answer before and after LLL basis reduction.
LLL returns a unimodular integer U such that B_reduced=B*U. The target stays
in ambient coordinates, and reduced-basis coefficients map back by z=U*z_reduced.
Babai uses rational Gram-Schmidt arithmetic with deterministic tie handling.
The unreduced-basis result is a diagnostic, not a competitive modern baseline.

Exact CVP is certified as follows. Any feasible incumbent gives a squared-cost
upper bound C. Every optimum obeys ||s||^2 <= C because this is a nonnegative
part of the total cost. Enumerate every integer s in that Euclidean ball.
For each s, minimize over w by taking the nearest q-multiple independently
in each coordinate. With odd q and integer t, this nearest choice is unique.
Evaluate the exact integer cost and keep every tied minimum. This covers all
global optima; the search bound comes from a public feasible solution, never
from the hidden planted secret. If the configured enumeration cap is exceeded,
the run fails explicitly instead of labelling an approximate answer exact.

4. Measurements and limits

Record separately: CVP cost and multiplicity, Babai optimality, planted-secret
recovery, whether the planted solution is optimal, basis transforms, and all
instance parameters. Keep all 64 consecutive seeds without filtering outcomes.
This is one exploratory development cohort, not a held-out statistical result.

There are no quantum circuits in EXP-001. Two bits per embedding coordinate
would nominally need 16 qubits, but neither coefficient bounds nor search
coverage are established. An eventual reduced/local encoding may change this.
The earlier 6/8-qubit generic pilot does not establish resources for this family.
