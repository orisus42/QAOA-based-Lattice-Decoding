EXP-008: IBM Fez routing and calibrated noise for QAOA lattice decoding
Internal research record, not final poster text or captions.

Purpose
  Test whether the full QAOA circuit and both previously selected 75% pruned
  variants preserve their resource/quality trade-off after physical connectivity,
  gate compilation, idle time, and calibrated device noise are included.
  The user also asks whether this project direction is promising before using
  the 600-second QPU allowance. This experiment submits no QPU jobs.

Calibration and physical mapping
  Live read-only metadata access found accessible IBM Fez, Kingston and
  Marrakesh backends. Fez was selected lexicographically without evaluating
  quantum solution outcomes. Public data were fetched around 05:00 local time
  on 20 October 2026; calibration last update was 03:37:33+05:30 the same day.
  Snapshot: research/calibrations/ibm_fez_20261020T050035937321+0530
  The capture utility saves public calibration properties and no credentials.
  It selects a connected 16-node patch from public connectivity/error values,
  preferring more internal edges, then lower mean CZ/readout error, then indices.
  Physical qubits: 91,92,93,94,95,98,99,109,110,111,112,113,114,115,118,129.
  The patch has 16 undirected edges, mean CZ error 0.0022293 and mean readout
  error 0.0173416. Native CZ duration is 68 ns, SX 24 ns, readout 1660 ns.
  T1 ranges 87.81--176.36 microseconds and T2 23.85--204.24 microseconds.
  Physical patch placement is fixed for all three methods and every instance.
  Initial logical bit i maps to patch position i. Qiskit optimization level 3
  and routing seed 2026102030 are shared. Final permutations are explicitly
  reversed before decoding the original lattice bitstring.
  Routing is constrained to this actual device subgraph. Allowing more device
  qubits or a different placement could change the result; no optimality claim.

Physics included and excluded
  Native rz/sx/x/cz/id gates are compiled to the calibrated target. Aer builds
  depolarizing plus gate-time thermal-relaxation channels from per-gate error,
  duration, qubit T1/T2 and frequency. T2 is capped at 2*T1 when needed.
  ALAP scheduling inserts explicit idle delays; a separate RelaxationNoisePass
  applies T1/T2 noise to delays only, avoiding duplicate gate-time noise.
  A final barrier aligns unitary completion. The simulator saves premeasurement
  physical probabilities; the calibrated asymmetric classical readout channel
  is then applied exactly on all physical bits, before mapping back to logical
  order. This preserves correct readout direction and physical calibration.
  The model assumes zero-temperature relaxation and perfect initial reset.
  It excludes leakage, crosstalk, calibration drift, coherent errors, correlated
  readout, mitigation, twirling, and dynamical decoupling. It is an approximate
  calibration-based model, not a measurement of real device performance.

Frozen study and computational shortcut
  D-020 freezes all choices before the main noisy evaluation. Full and both
  75% angle pairs are copied from EXP-007; no noise-aware fitting or selection.
  All 512 examples are routed and checked against the original ideal result.
  Babai already solves 441 exactly. Since its answer is always retained, the
  hybrid output for those cases remains optimal under ANY sampled distribution.
  Their noise cannot change the final hybrid quality. Therefore simulating all
  71 Babai-nonoptimal cases is sufficient for the full 512-case hybrid result.
  The reference labels are used solely to skip irrelevant evaluation work;
  they are not supplied to a solver or used to claim an execution speedup.
  For standalone QAOA behavior, use a separately seeded random 32-case subset.
  This subset contains 2 Babai failures and overlaps the 71-case diagnostic set.
  The union has 101 cases; never report this enriched union as a representative
  unconditional sample for QAOA-only performance. No methods or cases are
  dropped because of noisy outcomes, and no outcome-based stopping is used.

Noise trajectories, shot budgets, and uncertainty
  Exact 16-qubit density matrices would need about 64 GiB for the matrix alone.
  Instead, Aer samples 64 independent stochastic pure-state noise trajectories
  per circuit, saving the full probability distribution for each trajectory.
  This uses more distributional information than a single measured bitstring.
  It does not mean that only 64 physical measurement shots are being compared.
  Average these trajectories to estimate the mixed-state distribution, apply
  readout analytically, and compute estimated best-of-256/1024/4096/16384-shot
  metrics. These are noisy Monte Carlo plug-in estimates, not exact predictions.
  Store each trajectory's probabilities for costs up to the Babai incumbent,
  plus the full mean distributions before and after readout, seeds, and checksums.
  Every case uses all 64 prespecified trajectories. Main budget remains 1024.
  A 5000-replicate trajectory bootstrap gives fixed-cohort simulation uncertainty.
  A paired 512-group bootstrap combined with trajectory resampling separately
  quantifies instance-plus-simulation uncertainty. Methods share resampled groups.
  A leave-one-trajectory-out jackknife checks nonlinear plug-in bias; its
  correction is reported rather than silently relabeling estimates as exact.
  Scope remains exploratory local noise validation on the existing fresh cohort.

Reproducibility and source documentation
  Native routed circuits are stored as QPY for all 512 cases and three methods.
  QPY circuits use compact local patch indices 0--15. They are not directly
  submit-ready: map these indices back to the saved physical Fez qubit IDs,
  attach correctly mapped measurements, and validate against the refreshed
  full backend target before any separately authorized hardware execution.
  Each circuit's original ideal probability distribution is independently
  verified after routing and final-permutation correction, before adding noise.
  Physical CZ edges, gate counts, duration, delays, and calibrated noise models
  are saved. Each noisy output has a checkpoint with seed, time and SHA-256.
  Two preliminary timing profiles used old development MLWE-000 only, with no
  quality-based selection. The first exposed a global OpenMP thread cap; the
  second limited only BLAS threads and enabled Aer trajectory parallelism.
  Preliminary profiles are preserved under PRE-008 folders, separately from
  the main EXP-008 result. All physics and angle policies remain unchanged.
  A supplemental three-qubit synthetic circuit (not a Module-LWE performance
  result) cross-checks the complete gate/idle/readout model against exact
  density-matrix evolution. Its 4096-trajectory estimate differs by at most
  1.85 Monte Carlo standard errors across all eight probabilities. The model
  has a nonzero total-variation change of 0.0865 from the noiseless circuit.

QPU planning limits
  This study makes no cloud execution calls and uses zero QPU seconds.
  A possible later study compares 16 cases times three methods. Estimate time
  from mean scheduled duration plus readout, reset and default 250-us repetition
  delay, with one-job and one-job-per-circuit overhead scenarios. IBM documents
  approximately 2 seconds per sub-job, but decomposition/overhead can differ.
  These are planning estimates, not quota guarantees or an execution approval.
  Preserve at least 120 seconds of the user's 600-second budget. Refresh live
  calibration and actual remaining quota before any authorized QPU submission.
  Gate reduction can improve fidelity much more than wall-clock usage because
  repetition delay and other overheads dominate short circuits.

Results recorded 2026-10-20T05:31:14.868451+05:30
  EXP-008 replayed a live IBM Fez calibration from 20 October 2026 on a fixed connected 16-qubit patch. All 512 EXP-007 cases were routed with frozen full and both 75% QAOA angle policies; all 1536 routed circuits reproduce their ideal distributions. Mean native CZ gates fall from 559.752 to 165.549 (70.425%); mean scheduled unitary time falls from 22.783 to about 7.404 microseconds. Calibrated gate errors, gate and idle relaxation, and asymmetric readout were simulated using 64 noise trajectories per circuit. All 71 Babai-nonoptimal cases plus an independent random 32-case subset (101 unique cases) were simulated. Keeping Babai fixes the remaining 441 hybrid outputs at an exact optimum, so all 512 contribute to hybrid results. At 1024 shots the estimated expected hybrid success is 87.1373% for full, 88.4168% for 75%-same-angle, and 88.6458% for 75%-previously-trained; Babai alone is 86.1328%. Expected additional optima recovered are 5.143, 11.694, and 12.866. Paired success gains versus full are +1.2795 percentage points [0.9652,1.6236] and +1.5084 [1.1318,1.9097] (95% group-plus-simulation bootstrap intervals). The extra mean-distance benefit over Babai is 2.169 times [1.938,2.444] and 2.387 times [2.126,2.698] that of full noisy QAOA. At 16384 shots estimated hybrid success is 95.5957%, 98.7707%, and 98.9765%. These are Monte Carlo estimates under one approximate noise model, with separate simulation uncertainty and a finite-trajectory bias check, not observed hardware success rates. Classical two-coordinate refinement still solves 497/512 (97.0703%) with at most 513 candidate evaluations, beating all QAOA variants at the primary 1024-shot budget. The evidence supports reducing QAOA lattice-decoding resources and noise sensitivity on these tiny toy Module-LWE instances. No new tuning, real hardware execution, practical quantum advantage, secure ML-KEM attack, or novelty is claimed. Project QPU usage remains zero.

Additional uncertainty and validation details
  Fixed-cohort simulation-only 95% success intervals at 1024 shots:
    full: 87.1022--87.1726%; 75%-same: 88.3792--88.4518%;
    75%-trained: 88.6027--88.6850%.
  These quantify Monte Carlo uncertainty in expected success, not the variation
  of one future finite-shot hardware campaign, nor calibration/model error.
  Jackknife corrections to aggregate success at 1024 shots are +0.00087,
  +0.00117 and +0.00148 percentage points respectively; at 16384 they are
  +0.06657, +0.01762 and +0.01678 points. Reported estimates remain uncorrected
  with these bias diagnostics available. The correction is small relative to
  the paired differences; it does not establish exact noisy probabilities.
  The random 32-case standalone QAOA diagnostic gives estimated 1024-shot
  success of 8.6297%, 17.5947% and 18.3857%, respectively. Do not present these
  as standalone results over all 512 cases or over the enriched 101-case union.
  All 1536 routed ideal distributions agree to at most 5.36e-17 per probability.
  The independent saved-output audit checks 303 readout applications, 606
  best-of-shot statistics, and 12 full-cohort aggregates; all pass. All
  831 final output hashes were verified after figure generation.
  The 66 mathematical tests passed; installed-plugin deprecation warnings do
  not change the results. Routing and noise execution took about 17.9 minutes
  locally. This duration excludes later analysis and is not quantum runtime.

Interpretation and next work
  Both pruning variants have smaller circuits and better noisy aggregate
  quality than full QAOA at the primary budget, with positive paired intervals.
  The trained policy has the best point estimate, but this study does not
  establish its superiority over the same-angle 75% policy. The noisy follow-up
  is exploratory; intervals are not adjusted for all comparisons. Hardware
  model uncertainty is not contained in the bootstrap intervals.
  The finding supports an incremental QAOA implementation contribution and a
  focused hardware test. It does not beat strong classical decoding at the
  primary budget. Classical two-coordinate refinement solves 97.0703% with at
  most 513 evaluations; classical CBD sampling plus Babai is 97.0507% at 1024.
  At 16384 shots the pruned hybrid exceeds that deterministic refinement's
  exact-success rate, but uses a different sample/runtime budget; no overall
  speed or practical advantage follows.
  Gate reduction is about 70.4%; per-shot execution-cycle savings are only
  about 5.6% under the default 250-us repetition delay. Fidelity and quota
  savings are different claims. See hardware_plan_EXP008.txt for scenarios.
  Next, freeze a small paired hardware cohort without selecting pruning wins,
  refresh calibration/quota, verify physical mapping, and retain a 120-second
  reserve. No actual hardware cohort or job is created by this simulation.
