WORKING RESEARCH NOTES: CRYPTOGRAPHIC PROVENANCE AND PRUNING MECHANISMS
This is an internal explanation, not final poster prose or a caption.

1. What the ML-KEM connection now establishes

FIPS 203 K-PKE.KeyGen forms t_hat = A_hat*s_hat + e_hat in the NTT
representation of R_q = Z_q[x]/(x^256+1), q=3329. Converting back to
coefficient representation gives t = A*s + e modulo q. The public key
contains t_hat and the seed needed to regenerate A_hat. The secret and
key-generation error use CBD_eta1: eta1=3 for rank k=2 and eta1=2 for
k=3 or 4. Ciphertext compression, ciphertext noise and encapsulation are
outside our public-key decoding objective.

EXP-010 pinned kyber-py commit 897923667b2fa80afcf910fc8c1825dddf54a97d
and NIST ACVP-Server commit 975de31eb83d87039ec88934fdc47d8c312b892d.
All 75 supplied key-generation vectors (25 per parameter set) reproduced
the exact public and private key bytes. We decoded the coefficient arrays
and independently checked the equation using our NumPy negacyclic
convolution and signed-shift matrix expansion on all 75 full-size cases.
These are public test-vector secrets, used for verification only. This is
not formal implementation certification, and no full-size key was decoded
by our lattice solver or quantum algorithm.

Let A_c be the coefficient matrix. Our augmented basis and target are
  B = [[q I, A_c], [0, I]], y = [t, 0], z = [w, s].
Then ||Bz-y||^2 = ||q w + A_c s - t||^2 + ||s||^2.
For the planted key equation there is an integer wrap w giving residual
-e, hence cost ||e||^2+||s||^2. Minimizing this Euclidean cost favors a short
secret and error. It is not the exact discrete CBD likelihood. A closest
vector can encode another secret, particularly in a tiny ambiguous system.
Closest-vector success, planted-secret recovery and box coverage are
therefore different measurements. This models a toy key-recovery search;
it is not the operation performed by ordinary ML-KEM decapsulation.

Our quantum experiments independently generate lower-degree analogues,
not arbitrary cropped pieces of a secure ML-KEM key. They keep the
negacyclic polynomial/module equation and CBD sampling, but change degree,
rank and/or modulus and use a research RNG.
Changing degree and modulus also changes polynomial factorization and lattice
geometry; this is not a security-preserving parameter reduction.
The full ML-KEM degree is256; our n=2,k=2 experiments have only four scalar
secret coefficients. Under
the same two-bit-per-augmented-coordinate encoding, the illustrative
register would grow from 16 qubits to 2048/3072/4096 qubits at ML-KEM's
three ranks, before routing or improving the unproven search-window
coverage. These counts are not resource estimates for a successful attack.

EXP-010 evaluated 32 cases in each of six toy parameter families (192 total).
All boxes covered an optimum; all exact-reference searches completed.
The reference's bound was tightened by public exhaustive CBD-support
search, used only for evaluation. The QAOA center remained Babai's output.
Both q=3329 families and q=97 were already solved in all 32 cases by Babai.
Matching the secure modulus at tiny dimension does not establish useful
cryptanalytic difficulty. At q=17,eta=3, only 17/32 planted secrets belonged
to the optimum set; 75% pruning's optimum success was marginally below
full QAOA. These negative/null observations are retained.

2. Formal method and the finer sweep

LLL is applied to the public augmented basis. Babai supplies z0. Each
reduced-basis coefficient is restricted to z0_i+{-2,-1,0,1}, using two bits.
Expansion yields H = sum_i h_i Z_i + sum_(i<j) J_ij Z_i Z_j (constant
omitted). Coefficients use the original RMS normalization. Pruning removes
selected J terms only from the phase generator; fields, scoring objective,
Babai incumbent and original normalization are unchanged.

The p=1 circuit is exp(-i beta sum X) exp(-i gamma H_sparse)|+>^N.
The hybrid returns Babai or the lowest-original-cost sample, whichever is
better. It retains Babai on ties; elsewhere secret-return statistics use
the first sampled minimum. A zero-pair circuit is a product distribution
and is classically samplable.

EXP-011 tested all M+1 possible weakest-edge counts for every one of the
64 development cases: 6556 masks. Ties use deterministic coefficient-index
ordering. It also fitted 36 percentage settings plus one separate-field/
pair-angle control on the same independent 16 training groups. Each fit
received exactly 768 original-energy objective evaluations, with no
reference optimum or secret as an input. The optimizer deliberately ends
at a fixed budget, so its maximum-iterations status is not a convergence
guarantee. A further 19 control policies were evaluated, including random
and tied-magnitude permutations. Total statevectors: 10076.

At 1024 samples, expected hybrid optimum success on development data is:
  Babai alone                         82.8125%
  full QAOA                           86.9070%
  weakest75, unchanged angles          87.6504%
  weakest75, equally fitted angles     88.0772%
  weakest81, unchanged angles          88.0439%
  weakest81, equally fitted angles     88.7279%
  weakest90, equally fitted angles     87.1717%
At 16384 samples the highest tested common-grid success occurs at75%,
not81%, for both angle policies. These are exact finite-shot expectations
from ideal distributions, not observed percentages from hardware shots.
The grid maximum is exploratory and selected after many comparisons.
Pointwise bootstrap intervals do not establish a unique optimal fraction.
The original 512-group EXP-007/008 evidence still validates the previously
frozen75% settings; it is not fresh data for selecting81% or another rule.

3. What the ideal controls do and do not explain

At 1024 samples and original angles, count-matched75% deletion gives
87.6504% (weak),84.0762% (five random orders averaged within each case),
and83.3661% (strong). Which interactions are removed matters.

Weak75 removes16.19% of pair squared weight on average, retains7.92/8
within-coordinate bit interactions, and keeps the original ground states
near the bottom of the sparse spectrum (mean rank fraction0.00382).
Full/sparse energy rank correlation averages0.9417; the overlap of the
lowest1% of states is only47.0%, so the phase landscape does change.
Same-angle state fidelity averages0.8408. These are descriptive diagnostics,
not guarantees of improved sample quality.

Protecting coordinate pairs before removing weak terms gave the same
hybrid success as ordinary weak pruning on this cohort. Deleting these
pairs first gave83.5123%. However, these pairs are also strong interactions;
this comparison does not isolate coordinate geometry from magnitude.
There is no evidence yet that an explicit lattice-specific protection
rule improves over generic magnitude pruning.

Scaling every coupling to match weak75's retained squared pair weight,
without removing edges, gave86.5892%. A full circuit with separately
trained field and pair phase angles gave86.6776%, although it reduced the
training mean energy. These specific controls did not reproduce the gain.
They do not rule out all rescalings or better angle optimizers. Optimizing
mean energy and maximizing rare-optimum sampling are different objectives.

Randomizing only tied-magnitude removal gave87.6655%, close to the ordinary
tie order. Retaining90% of pair squared weight gave87.4006% with34.91 mean
RZZ terms, compared with25.36 forweak75 and101.44 forfull. This prior-art
style baseline also works; we have not established superiority to every
generic sparsifier. Removing every pair term gave83.3546%.

4. Noise mechanisms and numerical reliability

EXP-012 uses the same routed circuits for ideal, readout-only, gate-channel,
idle-only, gate+idle and gate+idle+readout comparisons. Gate channels already
include gate-duration T1/T2; idle relaxation covers scheduled delays.
Channel effects can interact and must not be added as independent losses.
All11 Babai failures are simulated; keeping the exact-optimal incumbent
fixes the other53 hybrid outputs without oracle access by the solver.
There are no full-cohort standalone-QAOA claims from this selected subset.

Two initial runs failed a strict trajectory-normalization check. Disabling
output truncation did not fix it. DIAG-013 reproduced a2.41456e-6 norm defect
with two-qubit Kraus fusion, versus1.04e-14 with one-qubit fusion and
9.33e-15 with fusion disabled, on the same routed circuit/seed. The new
noise run uses one-qubit fusion throughout, without renormalizing outputs
or loosening checks. This identifies a numerical association, not a full
upstream root-cause diagnosis. Failed runs and diagnostic arrays remain.

The successful run simulated 66 circuits with 128 trajectories each (8448
trajectories), plus 22 ideal routed checks. Maximum trajectory norm error
was 8.66e-11, within the unchanged 1e-10 absolute tolerance. No probability
renormalization was performed. Independently reloaded all 22 QPY circuits,
checked 8168 native CZ edges, and verified 264 finite-shot statistics with
the older independent calculation.

At 1024 samples, full versus weak75 expected hybrid success (%) was:
  ideal                 86.90696   87.65038
  readout only          86.32468   87.29215
  gate channels         84.99203   86.75260
  idle only             85.49397   87.25791
  gate + idle           84.34856   86.45633
  gate + idle + readout  84.16474   86.20209
The combined-noise paired gain is 2.03735 percentage points, with a
pointwise 95% group-plus-Monte-Carlo bootstrap interval [0.92232,3.31987].
The ideal difference is 0.74342 points; its 3000-resample interval in this
run barely includes zero, unlike the similarly borderline estimate from
the different bootstrap seed in EXP-011. Do not call the small development
ideal difference conclusively significant. The combined-noise result is
clearer under this model. Neither interval covers model misspecification.

Both gate channels and idle relaxation increase the point-estimated
benefit of pruning. Their effects are not additive; readout changes the
gap differently depending on the preceding state/noise. Shorter circuits
and altered state sensitivity remain intertwined. These experiments do
not prove that gate count alone explains every difference.

At 16384 samples with combined noise the estimates are 94.8890% full and
99.1499% weak75. Finite-trajectory plug-in bias is small in the recorded
jackknife check (maximum absolute estimate across all channels/budgets
0.0577 percentage points). These remain Monte Carlo predictions rather
than hardware observations. This 64-case development study does not
replace the previous frozen 512-case evaluation.

5. Contribution boundaries

Existing work already sparsifies QAOA phase operators (Liu et al.,2022)
and uses magnitude/top-K/squared-weight compression (Cavaliere et al.,2026).
The current contribution is an application-specific, reproducible study of
the quality/resource trade-off in short-secret module-derived lattice
decoding, with explicit cryptographic provenance, strong classical controls,
sparsity/budget curves, negative controls, and calibrated noise components.
No new pruning algorithm, universal mechanism, optimal75% setting, quantum
advantage or secure ML-KEM attack has been demonstrated. A mathematical
proof of improved low-energy sampling and independence from the particular
basis, encoding, p=1 restriction and problem family remain open.

The evidence can support a useful student methods/benchmark project even
when classical decoding wins. A stronger novelty claim would require a
specific new method plus a fair comparison and independent validation.
Final hardware selection should favor a stable resource/quality region,
retain75% as the historical anchor, and validate any new selection on
untouched groups before using the600-second QPU budget (120-second reserve).
No QPU seconds have been used.
